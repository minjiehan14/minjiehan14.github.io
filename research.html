Should News Organizations Share Data with Generative AI?
With Jinyang Zheng and Huaxia Rui · [Accepted at Management Science]
Abstract

The longer-run evolution of digital content markets under generative AI depends in part on how incumbent content providers respond strategically. While prior research documents the immediate and short-term disruptions caused by generative AI, we examine the incremental effects that emerge when content providers adapt. We study data-sharing agreements between OpenAI and 18 major news outlets to assess how cooperation involving access to incremental and exclusive content affects news consumption. Using the generalized synthetic control method, we estimate the effect of cooperation on news-site traffic and find a positive effect on visits. The increase is concentrated in unique visitors rather than deeper engagement among existing users, consistent with generative AI expanding the reach of news content to additional audiences. Our mechanism analyses leverage the contrast between attributive and non-attributive cooperation and document patterns consistent with search-cost reduction at two levels: the content level, through direct attribution links to news articles, and the brand level, through an advertising effect that increases user awareness and interest. The results exhibit limited heterogeneity across other outlet characteristics and little change in average user engagement. These findings offer timely insights into ongoing debates over cooperation and conflict between content providers and generative AI developers while informing a broader understanding of their strategic interaction.

Breaking the Sound Barrier: Asymmetric Impacts of AI-Powered Auto-Dubbing on Multilingual Engagement on YouTube
[Mikhail Lysyakov and Yang Gao] · [Major Revision at MIS Quarterly]
Abstract

The global video content market faces significant language barriers that hinder broader audience engagement. YouTube, with over 2.49 billion monthly active users, is globally consumed but contains a large share of English-language content, even though only about 18% of the global population speaks English. This mismatch creates language frictions in both directions: non-English speakers face barriers to engaging with English videos, and English speakers might find non-English content less accessible. To break such language barriers, YouTube launched an AI-powered auto-dubbing feature in December 2024 to automatically translate and synthesize speech across languages. Leveraging this rollout as a quasi-natural experiment, we examine the impact of auto-dubbing on user engagement using data from 5,000 channels between October 15, 2024, and March 17, 2025. Using staggered difference-in-differences with propensity score matching, we find that auto-dubbing increases engagement, particularly comments from viewers in dubbed languages, by 9.42%. However, the effect is asymmetric: English-language channels benefit significantly more than non-English channels. Mechanism analysis reveals that content complexity amplifies the effect, consistent with reduced cognitive load, while creators’ emotional expressiveness weakens it, reflecting current AI limitations in preserving vocal nuance. The increase in engagement is driven by new commenters rather than existing multilingual users. These findings contribute to media richness and cultural proximity theories, highlighting that the effectiveness of auto-dubbing depends on language dominance, content features, and emotional delivery. Practically, they suggest the need for culturally sensitive and emotion-aware AI localization strategies.
